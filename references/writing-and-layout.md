# Writing and Layout Review

Use for new CVs and substantial revisions. This guide supplements the evidence map and offline checks; it does not change search scores, application permissions or source precedence. Candidate details, examples from real applicants and personal preferences stay in the Jobs root, never this reusable guide.

## Build a Recruiter's Case

Extract the ad's principal deliverables, explicitly essential criteria and desirable criteria. Repetition is a signal of emphasis, not proof a criterion is mandatory. Choose the strongest supported connections and record each as direct employment evidence, transferable work, project/study evidence or a gap. Explain why the connection matters without converting it into experience the candidate has not had.

Draft the short summary last. It should identify the candidate's actual professional background, supply specific evidence of relevance and explain the transition where needed. Avoid adjective-heavy openings, a list of every degree, invented motivations or adopting the target job title as an already-held identity. Put the strongest relevant evidence near the top; use clear experience groupings if older relevant work precedes newer supporting experience, with dates and employers intact.

Use standard section names and choose sections for this role. A technical role may need projects and a grouped technical-skills section; a legal-support role may need more document and client evidence and less coding detail. Do not mechanically remove Office/Excel if relevant or describe unverified proficiency as advanced. Do not hide internships, project-only experience, months or employment gaps.

Keep each degree's grade, distinction and completion status attached to that degree. An academic qualification is not professional registration or membership; use wording that cannot imply either. A missing source entry is not proof of no experience, so do not add unsupported negative self-descriptions. Future learning or willingness belongs to a confirmed aspiration, not past delivery.

## Write Substantive Bullets

Prefer action + specific object/context + supported contribution or result. Each bullet should contribute a distinct fact. Select a verb matching actual responsibility: assisted, reviewed, tested and coordinated can be stronger than an unsupported led, owned or transformed.

- Cut filler such as "maintaining a careful approach" when the actual control or document already demonstrates care.
- Replace broad virtues with concrete work products, stakeholders, tools or decisions supported by sources.
- Do not append an invented result to every duty. A precise responsibility is better than an unsupported achievement.
- Separate outputs from outcomes: producing a report is not proof of faster decisions; contributing code is not ownership of a platform; testing is not verified defect reduction.
- Vary emphasis rather than rewriting the same fact in summary, skills and several bullets. Use concise skills labels supported by experience rather than duplicate mini-paragraphs.
- Usually aim for one or two rendered lines per bullet, allowing a third where the evidence genuinely needs it. Edit before shrinking type.

Numbers are optional. Include them only with a source, timeframe and attributable scope. A user-confirmed approximation may retain its qualifier after checking its basis; never manufacture a conservative estimate. Do not turn total tenure into specialist years, a team result into an individual result, a weekly observation into an annual total without confirmed representativeness, or an unknown improvement into a percentage. Ask focused questions privately when missing evidence would materially strengthen the case; continue without the number when unanswered.

## Keyword and Parse Review

Map important ad terms to real evidence and natural locations in the document. Use a term only if it accurately describes the work. Preserve distinctions such as intellectual-property compliance versus financial-services regulation, or case coordination versus project-budget ownership. Unknown systems remain gaps, not keywords to insert. No hidden text or keyword stuffing.

Check actual text extraction and reading order, visible headings, identity, dates and employment blocks in both Word and PDF. These are local structural checks, not a test of the employer's ATS. A keyword-coverage measure, if requested, must explain its method and must not be called an ATS pass probability, hiring score or interview likelihood. Do not repeat unsupported general rejection statistics or guarantee compatibility with all systems.

## Visual Direction

Respect configured reference documents while making deliberate improvements where the user has requested them. Follow the current document/PDF skill's applicable constraints if these illustrative style choices conflict. For professional operational roles, prefer a polished single-column A4 document: a clear name/contact block in the body, differentiated section and employer headings, compact role/date lines, aligned bullets and consistent spacing. A restrained dark accent can distinguish section labels where permitted, but body text must remain legible in greyscale. Avoid photos, sidebars, icons, skill bars, text boxes and layout tables for essential content.

Use a small, consistent type scale, usually 10.5-11.5 pt body, 12-13 pt section headings and 22-26 pt name, adjusted to the reference and readable output. Maintain comfortable margins, line spacing and a visible distinction between roles. Keep the title black and avoid ornamental title rules. All essential details belong in the body; a simple optional page-number footer is not career evidence.

Balance the two-page composition by editing and moving complete role/section blocks, not stretching text or adding irrelevant bullets. Do not force a break that leaves most of a page empty. Conversely, do not compress valuable evidence just to fit one page. Inspect each page at normal reading scale as well as checking technical defects: can a recruiter locate the relevant history quickly, distinguish dates and employers, and see why this person fits without reading every line?

## Cover Letters

Only create when requested under the main skill's rules. Match the CV's typography. Use a specific, accurate connection to the advertised work, two or three supporting examples and a natural close. Neither an elaborate hook nor a metric is compulsory. Do not invent company research, personal connections, passion, ongoing courses or reasons for leaving. Do not bury the case in disclaimers, internal evidence language or a repetition of every CV section.

## Editorial Gate and Version Control

Record a short internal review against the finished files, not a universal score:

1. Relevance: the strongest supported reasons to shortlist the candidate are apparent on page one.
2. Substance: each bullet adds specific evidence; outcomes, scope and verbs are defensible.
3. Economy: no stock padding, unnecessary repetition or inflated labels.
4. Presentation: coherent hierarchy, readable body text, balanced pages and correct extracted order.
5. Integrity: sources, dates, titles, qualifications and gaps remain truthful; private declarations stay out of the CV.

Fix concrete weaknesses before ready status. This editorial judgment complements, never replaces, hash-bound source review, every-page visual inspection and a passing offline report. Preserve original sources and already-submitted versions. Save revisions as new artifacts; record which version is preferred for a pending application, and recheck before future upload. Generated documents are derivative artifacts, never independent factual sources.
